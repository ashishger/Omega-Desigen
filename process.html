Design Process

A structured workflow that makes your planning journey clear and predictable.

We divide every project into transparent stages so you can review, decide, and move forward confidently without confusion.

What This Gives You

  • Clear milestones and expected deliverables
  • Focused review points before finalization
  • Better cost and timeline control
  • Smoother transition from design to construction

01

Requirement Discussion

We collect details on plot size, number of floors, family needs, style preference, and budget intent.

02

Site and Feasibility Review

Orientation, access, light flow, and design constraints are studied to shape practical options.

03

Concept Planning

Initial 2D layout directions are shared for discussion and early alignment.

04

Design Refinement

Selected concept is developed with room-level details, circulation clarity, and functional improvements.

05

3D and Style Detailing

Facade and interior-exterior visual direction are finalized for better confidence before execution.

06

Final Package and Support

Final drawing references and guidance are delivered for approvals and construction coordination.

Typical Deliverables

What you receive during the process.

  • Requirement and design briefing notes
  • 2D layout development and refined plan set
  • 3D visual references for elevation direction
  • Interior and exterior styling guidance
  • Support for approval-ready design presentation

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers before you start.

How long does a typical project take?

Timeline depends on scope, but most residential planning projects move through core stages within a few weeks with timely feedback.

Can I request revisions after concept sharing?

Yes. Revisions are part of our process and are handled at defined checkpoints so changes stay organized.

Do you help with design approvals?

Yes. We provide support in preparing design documentation aligned to approval presentation needs.

Do I need to finalize interior before starting layout planning?

No. We can begin with layout priorities first and develop interior direction progressively.
